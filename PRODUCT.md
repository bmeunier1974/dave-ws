# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML/CSS, no build step. `index.html` (French) and `en/index.html` (English). `lang.js` picks the language. No framework.

## Users

Homeowners on the South Shore of Montreal (Longueuil, Brossard, Greenfield Park area). They want a kitchen, bathroom, basement, extension or new build done. They look for a contractor they can trust, check the RBQ licence, and ask for a free quote by phone or email.

## Product Purpose

One-page site for Dave & co, a new construction and renovation contractor. It tells who Dave & co is, what they do, where they work, and how to get a free quote. Success: the visitor calls or emails for a quote.

## Positioning

Renovation first (kitchen, bathroom, basement, interior), with extensions and new builds as further services. Local, licensed South Shore contractor.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- French first (Charter of the French language / Bill 96). English is an extra page, never a replacement.
- RBQ licence number must show: `Licence RBQ : XXXX-XXXX-XX`. No RBQ logo, no Quebec flag.
- Law 25: no form, no tracking cookies. Short privacy section with the privacy contact.
- Contact only by `tel:` and `mailto:` links.
- All missing facts use the marker `[À CONFIRMER : ...]` so `grep "À CONFIRMER"` finds them.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: "Dave & co". No logo yet.
- Visual reference given by the owner: a modern home-builder landing page (big serif display headline, warm taupe and brown, landscape photo hero, floating rounded cards). Binding as the visual reference.

## Evidence on Hand

- No real project photos yet: the company is new. The owner chose free stock photos (Unsplash) in the "Réalisations" grid and hero, shown without a label, until real photos exist. Each stock photo is marked in the source for later replacement.
- No real testimonials, years in business, numbers, or memberships. Do not invent them; keep the `[À CONFIRMER]` markers.

## Product Principles

1. Trust before style: licence, insurance and contact facts are always easy to find.
2. French first, plain words.
3. Never invent a number, review, or claim.
4. One clear action: ask for a free quote.
